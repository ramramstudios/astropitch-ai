How it works

The mapping

The original AstroPitch database gave every sign a pitch: Aries A 440, Taurus A♯, Gemini B, and so on up to Pisces G♯. That is a chromatic scale laid around a circle — twelve signs of 30° against twelve semitones. So the conversion is not a lookup table, it is arithmetic:

frequency = A × 2(longitude / 360)

One trip around the zodiac is one octave. A planet at 14°22′ Aries is not "an A" — it is an A raised by 29 cents, and you can hear it.

Why aspects are intervals

If 30° is a semitone, then every classical aspect is an interval, with no extra assumptions:

    The tritone falling on the opposition is a nice accident. So is the trine landing on a major third.

    What makes the timbre

    A note in the database was sign + house, and one of its rows reads "aries = A, house = timbre". That is the whole design:

    • Sign → pitch. Continuous in ecliptic longitude.
    • Element → material. The oscillator stack, harmonic content, noise layer and body resonance. Fire is dense and driven, Earth is low-order and wooden, Air is hollow with audible breath, Water is detuned and never still.
    • House → gesture. How the material is played: the 2nd is struck, the 5th sings, the 6th runs to a clock, the 7th is doubled, the 11th is an inharmonic bell, the 12th arrives before it begins.
    • Modality → phrasing. Cardinal strikes and moves on, fixed holds, mutable wavers.
    • Planet → register and role. Saturn two octaves down carrying structure, Mercury an octave up, the Sun, Moon and Ascendant loudest.

    The astronomy

    Positions are computed in the browser, with no server and no API. The Sun and Moon follow Meeus (the Moon with its full 60-term longitude series); the planets use JPL's approximate Keplerian elements, good to well under a degree from 1800 to 2050. Houses are whole sign, equal, or Placidus by semi-arc division, falling back to Porphyry inside the polar circles where Placidus has no solution.

    The sound

    Raw Web Audio, no library. One signal graph is built once and reused: a glue compressor, soft-clip saturation, a synthetic stereo reverb whose tail darkens as it decays, a ping-pong delay with a damped feedback loop, and a limiter. Each note allocates only its own oscillators and gains, and tears them down when it finishes.
